Floor Board — Design Assessment

2026-08-11 · board v2.1
rendered from the live file

Method: the real FloorBoard.html (6,212 lines, read fresh) rendered in headless Chromium against a mock shift built to exercise every feature — order bands, kit threads, shelf counts, a deviance, a floor note, dual shelves, pack sizes, caps, paid shipping. Four viewports: wall 1920×1080, tablet landscape 1280×800 (touch), tablet portrait 800×1280 (touch), phone 390×844. Prayer/weather deliberately offline — blanks there are mock artifacts, not findings. The render loop is now permanent: design-lab/render.js.

The verdict

The bones are right. The presentation is failing them — and your instinct is correct on all three counts. "Hard to read" is a chrome tax: on the tablet, the first pickable row starts 36% of the way down the screen, and in landscape the pick list — the board's entire job — shows half of one clipped row. "Inconsistencies" are measurable: 31 distinct font sizes and 117 distinct color literals in one stylesheet, with the same screen position carrying four different meanings. "Style bugs" are real and reproducible: colliding hero labels, numbers clipped off the phone's edge, three CSS variables that were never defined.

None of this is fixed by nudging pixels. The board is four layouts sharing one DNA, and the tool keeps inheriting the monitor's furniture. My recommendation at the end: design the tablet as THE surface, fold kit progress into the order bands, tuck everything ambient behind one drawer — and give the wall its own simple projection or retire it. One decision is yours to make first.

What the loop sees

The board today, four ways

Findings · ranked by harm

What is actually wrong

F1

Landscape starves the pick list to half a row

Broken

1280×800 · everything above the fold is chrome; the one visible row is cut mid-pixel

The stacked portrait flow is reused in landscape, so the vertical budget dies before the list begins, and the feed panel then claims what's left. A picker who rotates the tablet — the most natural gesture there is — gets a board that shows nothing to pick.

Fix Landscape must use the width: the pick list owns the left ~65% at full height; everything ambient lives in a right rail or disappears. No stacked panels below 900px of height.

F2

The chrome tax — the job starts a third of the way down

Structural

portrait · five bands of furniture between the clock and the first shelf

Header (two rows — the prayer chip owns a centered line of its own) → hero strip → paid strip → panel title → kit strip → channel tabs. Six layers, ~460px, before shelf A-14 appears. Every layer is individually defensible; together they bury the reason the board exists. For a non-technical picker, the screen should answer "where do I walk next?" in the first glance, not the fourth.

Fix One top bar (brand · clock · to-grab count · paid badge · live dot). Tabs become the panel header. Kit strip dissolves into the bands (F5). Paid shipping becomes a badge that opens detail on tap rather than a permanent 78px strip.

F3

The wall shows half the floor, plus buttons nobody can press

Structural

1920×1080 · eleven eBay lines fill the panel; DIRECT never appears; every row has a touch button

The monitor layout stacks both channels in one scroller — but a wall is never scrolled, so on any busy day the DIRECT table simply does not exist on the wall. And the tool's ✓ PICK buttons render on a display with no finger, taking the row's most prominent slot for a dead control.

Fix A monitor is a different projection, not a bigger tool: both channels side-by-side, always visible, no buttons, bigger type. This is the fork in the Directions section.

F4

The hero strip cannot fit its own arithmetic

Broken

portrait & landscape · the label runs into its number; cell widths are content-blind

phone · "43+" exits the viewport; the card then holds ~80px of nothing

These are the "style bugs" you feel. The tool hero compresses five cards into one strip with fixed proportions, so at real content sizes labels collide and numbers clip. On the phone the same strip becomes a card with overflow on one side and dead space on the other.

Fix The tool needs numbers chosen for the tool, not the wall's five cards squeezed. One number (to grab) in the top bar; the rest live in the Today drawer (F8).

F5

The kit strip sits in the wrong place and says things twice

Structural

Three rows of the most premium space — above even the channel tabs — narrate DIRECT kits while you are on the eBay tab, describing work that isn't on screen. Switch to DIRECT and the strip duplicates what the bands already say: KIT-217475 · left: A-51 · B-44 on the strip, and SO-24618 · A-51 · B-44 · D-7 on the shut card three rows below it.

Fix Completeness is a fact about an order → it belongs ON the order band: SO-24618 · 3 lines · KIT 2 of 5 · 6h 40m. Spines stay on rows. The strip disappears, and ~116px returns to the list.

F6

The left edge means four different things

Consistency

On today's board the row's left edge can be: a kit thread (cyan/violet spine) · a floor-note accent · a deviance tint · a red age accent on bands. Same position, four vocabularies. A non-technical picker can never learn what "a colored edge" means, because it doesn't mean anything — it means four things. This violates the board's own written rule: one meaning per color.

Fix Reserve the spine for kit membership only. Notes get their 📌 line (already do); deviance gets its red chip in the count lane (already has one — the row tint is the duplicate); age lives on the band's right side as text.

F7

31 font sizes · 117 colors · zero system

Consistency

Mechanical audit of the stylesheet: 31 distinct px font sizes (9, 9.5, 10, 10.5, 11, 11.5, 12, 12.5, 13 … 80) — ten of them below 12px on a tablet read at arm's length — and 30 hex + 87 rgba() color literals. The "same" gray is a dozen different grays. There are also three separate @media (max-width: 620px) blocks scattered through the cascade — the exact source-order trap that has already bitten this file twice. This is why every change feels like it breaks something else: there is no system to stay inside.

Fix The rules table below — 5 type sizes, ~12 color tokens, one block per breakpoint. Adopt it first, then every later fix inherits it.

F8

Rows change height; the scan rhythm breaks

Consistency

portrait · a counted + noted row balloons to 3 lines between 1-line neighbours

A row with a count, a deviance and a floor note grows to triple height and shouts across the list — even though the picker didn't select it. Scanning a list depends on rhythm; every tall row is a stutter. The count lane's micro-copy (on hand 4 → 2 left, 11px gray) also sits below comfortable reading size for the floor.

Fix Fixed two-lane row grid: lane one shelf/sku/qty/action, lane two (constant height when present) count + note. Compress the count verdict to its chip.

F9

Undefined and dead CSS tokens

Polish

--green-glow and --red-glow are referenced and never defined (known), and the audit found a third: --rule-strong. Properties using them silently fall back — borders and glows that "should" be there simply aren't, which is exactly the "style bug" texture you're seeing. --brand-deep and --ink-soft are defined and never used.

Fix Define or delete — 10 minutes, part of adopting the token table.

F10

The tool carries monitoring content it never shows

Structural

Shipped/received live in the hero, again in the feed, again in the rest ledger. On the tablet the feed is below the fold anyway — the tool pays the chrome (panel headers, counts) for content a picker doesn't consult mid-walk. Ambient information is the monitor's job.

Fix One collapsed "Today" drawer at the bottom of the tool: tap it for shipped / received / feed. The monitor keeps them permanently visible — that's the difference between the two roles, made structural.

Credit where due

What already works — keep these

The DIRECT accordion One box open at a time, shut cards that still name their shelves, oldest auto-opened. The board's best screen — the target quality bar.
Channel tabs = one job at a time Pickers don't work both channels at once; the tabs made that structural. Counts report floor truth, not the cap.
Shelf-first rows The location is the biggest thing on the row — correct, it's the routing key. Dual shelves as two tokens; pack-size receding: both right.
✓ Pick at 44px The only write action is the only button, sized for a thumb. PREP marks the exception, not the norm.
Honest arithmetic Bands count the whole order (can't show a fragment as whole); "+N not shown" admits the cap; the count lane shows its working and names "spoken for".
The beacon & the accordion's memory rules Names the aisle, clears on action, never auto-collapses what a person opened, nothing persists on a shared tablet.

The fork

Three directions — pick one

All three fix F1/F4/F9 (the outright breakage). They differ in how much structure moves, and in what happens to the wall.

A · Repair in place

≈ 1–2 sessions · lowest risk · keeps today's structure

Fix the landscape budget, the hero collisions, the phone overflow, the undefined tokens; trim the header to one row. The board looks like today, minus the bugs.

Honest cost: the chrome tax, the duplicated kit strip, the four-meaning left edge and the 31-size type zoo all remain. It polishes a crowded skeleton — you will likely be back here in a month.

Recommended

B · Tool-first restructure

≈ 3–4 sessions incl. lab rounds · the design becomes simple because the structure is

Design the 800×1280 tablet as the surface. Everything a picker doesn't act on mid-walk leaves the walking screen:

C · Split the roles (the wall question)

≈ +1 session on top of B · removes the 4-layout tax permanently

The monitor stops sharing the tool's DNA: /?view=wall renders both channels side-by-side, no buttons, giant type, feed visible — a true projection. The tool then never has to compromise for the wall again, which is the class of work that made the last two sessions a grind.

The decision that's yours: is the wall/desk-monitor role still load-bearing — is a screen actually mounted or watched that way today? If yes → B + C. If the tablet is the only real surface → B alone, and we delete the monitor layout entirely, which is even simpler.

Write once, stop re-deriving

The proposed design rules

Whichever direction you pick, these get written into the lab file first — every later change inherits them instead of re-inventing them.

RuleValueWhy
Type scale 34 · 20 · 16 · 13 · 11 px, nothing else hero number · shelf · row data · secondary · caps-labels. Replaces 31 sizes. Nothing below 11px, and 11px is labels only.
Color = meaning yellow = act now · green = confirm/done · red = disagreement or past-deadline · amber = money caution · kit threads only One meaning per color, no exceptions. The CVD-safe kit pair stays exactly as searched.
Position = meaning left spine = kit membership · right column = the action · band = the box Kills the four-meaning left edge (F6).
Spacing 8px scale: 8 / 16 / 24 / 32 Gaps group; ink doesn't. The accordion already proves it.
Row anatomy one fixed grid, two lanes max Constant rhythm; the exception fits the lane or becomes a chip (F8).
Tokens only ~12 color tokens, all in :root, zero literals in rules 117 literals today. An undefined var becomes impossible to miss (F9).
Cascade one media block per breakpoint, last in the file Three scattered 620px blocks today; this trap has bitten twice already.
Minimums touch 44px · text 11px · one sticky element per column The board's own hard-won rules, promoted from comments to law.

Next steps

How this proceeds

  1. You pick the direction (A / B / B+C) and answer the wall question — one sentence is enough.
  2. Wireframes first — ASCII rounds like the split and the accordion, which were both decided in seconds. No CSS until the skeleton is signed.
  3. Lab file, side-by-side variants — 2–3 candidates rendered in one image through this loop; you look once and point.
  4. Port to FloorBoard.html, re-render all four viewports, diff against the lab, then one scp + hard-reload on the devices.
