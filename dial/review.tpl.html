Row One,
Restructured

Seven structures for the masthead, every one rendered at true 1:1 against the real table — because the last round was reviewed at 2–3× and shipped at 1×, and that is exactly how a roundel that had already been predicted to fail at 14 discs got shipped anyway.

What I found before drawing anything: the current look has two independent causes, and only one of them is about structure.

The board is composed at grid resolution — “MOTOR SERVICE” is set at 13px inside a 14-row canvas, then one pixel per disc decides lit or unlit. And the OFF disc is #26221c on a ground that starts at #26221c, so the unlit discs are invisible and you never see a board — only sparse dots on black.

Fix those two and row one improves a great deal without moving a single cell. Whether that is enough is the question this page is for.

And then you named the real one. The band is #1a1a1a flat. The art's ground is a gradient #26221c → #141210 → #100e0c — three blacks the sheet has never had — with a fade only on its right edge. That is the pasted-on block you can see, and I had it filed as a minor cohesion fix. It is the headline.

One correction since the first draft. My first renders stacked a small tracked label over a big number inside one cell. A cell holding a formula cannot do that — rich text does not survive a recalc, and every readout here has to update itself with no trigger. One size, weight and colour per cell; two lines are fine, two sizes are not. Every render below now obeys that, so what ships will look like what you are seeing.

One idea I tried and ruled out, so nobody suggests it later: inverting the board — lit becomes unlit — is impossible here. Unlit discs are the band colour and are never drawn, so an inverted frame lights the whole rectangle and the blend breaks for the length of the effect.

What “pitch” means, and can a flag survive here

Pitch is the gap between disc centres — so a smaller pitch means more, smaller discs, and a finer mark. It is the only thing it changes. The magnified row below is the whole idea: at 3px the discs are big and the letterforms come out stepped; at 2px the curves close up. Nothing else about the design moves.

And your flag idea — the instinct is right, the flag is the wrong vehicle. A flag is its colours, and this board has one accent. Rendered in a single tone the stars and stripes become a striped rectangle with a lighter block, and the Texas flag loses the blue/white/red split that is the whole of its identity. Both are on the sheet so you can judge rather than take my word.

The lone star is the better version of your own idea. One shape, unmistakable at any size, and it says Texas more elegantly than fifty stars can at 26 disc rows.

And then 2px turned out to be impossible

I recommended 2px off the concept sheet. It does not survive encoding. The concept tiles were PNGs; a GIF is quantised, and at 2px a disc is 1.62px across on a 1.73px row spacing — adjacent discs merge under rasterisation and the board stops reading as discs at all. It becomes solid streaks.

And the limit is geometric, not the palette. I tested 6, 12 and 20 colours at 2px: identical failure, because the problem is that there is no gap left to draw. That is worth writing down — it would be the obvious thing to try next.

2.5px is the floor: 61 disc rows across the mark against 3px's 51, and the discs still resolve. That is what shipped.

Does any of this touch the quota?

No. Not the Apps Script one, not eBay's, not Zoho's.

The art is a static file on your VPS, served by Caddy. Apps Script touches it exactly once — when you run installRowOneNow(), it fetches the file and hands it to Sheets. That is an editor action, not a trigger, so it never draws on the ~90 minutes a day of trigger runtime. n8n, the Floor Board and the published tick never see it at all.

What it actually costs is two things. Sheets stores the image inside the spreadsheet, so opening the sheet downloads it once per session — 0.62 MB is about a second on decent wifi, then the browser caches it. And it costs a little device CPU while it animates, which matters more on the warehouse tablet than the bytes do.

So the number that matters is not the file size — it is how much of the loop is moving. A GIF frame carries its own delay, so during a fifteen-second still the browser decodes one frame and waits: near-zero cost. That is the real argument for the resting board, and it means I can spend length on stillness almost for free and only pay for the seconds that move.

The two things that fixed “not smooth”

1 · A disc now rotates instead of swapping. The old transition threw each disc from one state to the other in a single frame — that is not a flip, it is a swap. A real flip-disc turns about a horizontal axis: it passes through edge-on, where it is a thin line, and shows its other face on the way out. Its projected height is |cos θ| as θ sweeps to π, and the face changes at the quarter turn. Frame rate went 10–12fps to 24.

2 · Only discs that change flip, and the front is staggered. A real board leaves the rest alone — and an unchanged disc gives the encoder nothing to carry, so it is cheaper as well as truer. Each disc takes its cue from the wave plus a deterministic jitter, so the front reads as a board catching up with itself rather than a ruler being dragged across it.

Two causes, not one

Measured

Same content, same width, same 56px height in all four. Rows ① and ② are the shipped renderer; ③ and ④ area-sample the composition at full pixel size, the way a real display resolves an image.

Can you see where the image is?

The brief

Row one should read as one unbroken black band, and the discs should simply appear out of it. That makes the test binary: put the art on the real band colour and look for the rectangle. The caret above each strip marks where the image actually starts and stops, so you can check your eye against the truth.

This reverses one of my own recommendations. I had argued for visible unlit discs — they are what makes a flip-disc board read as a physical object. But a physical object sitting on the sheet is exactly what you do not want, and the visible grid is what draws the rectangle. Legibility turned out to come from pitch and sampling, not from seeing the unlit discs, so nothing is lost by hiding them.

The bug underneath it

Matching the ground is not enough on its own, because the encoder moves it. rgb565 cannot represent #1a1a1a — it keeps five bits of red and blue, so 0x1a (26) lands on 24 or 27. Measured on the decoded frames: the rail's ground came back #1b1b1b and the block's came back #26251e — twelve levels out, which is the pasted-on rectangle all over again. Nothing reports it; only sampling a decoded frame finds it.

Fixed by snapping the palette entry nearest the ground to the exact band colour after quantising. Verified across every file and every frame.

The board should rest

Verified

A real Solari board sits still and then flips — the charm is the event. Ours never stops, which is most of why it reads as a screensaver rather than an instrument.

I assumed resting would be a trade against something. It is not. A long hold is one frame. GIF carries a per-frame delay up to 655 seconds, so “rest for nine seconds” costs a single frame, not seventy-two. Resting is the cheapest thing the format can do.

Does the eBay mark go in the GIF?

Your question

No — float it on top, in every one of the three. Two images: the board first, the mark second. Three reasons.

  • Rendered into discs, the eBay mark goes monochrome. Those four colours are the entire recognisability of that logo — a grey disc version of it is just a word.
  • Baked in, it is stuck. Floated, the cell keeps its formula and the mark can move, resize or leave without re-rendering a 150 KB file.
  • A mark sitting on an object reads as a plate on a machine. Beside it, as a neighbour. On is what you want.

The real cost of E and F is not the logo's colour — it is that eBay's label stops having a band of its own. In C it keeps one. That is the whole difference, and it is why I still land on C.

Should the DIRECT band be a GIF too?

Your question

No — and the first reason is technical, not taste.

  • That row moves. Every floating image in this project anchors at A1 or D1 — inside the frozen pane, on a row that can never shift. The DIRECT divider is found by scanning column A for the word precisely because its row number changes: there are 21 insert and delete sites that move it, including n8n's nightly sweep of shipped rows. An image anchored there drifts off the band, or is orphaned when its row is deleted.
  • Motion belongs where nobody is reading. The masthead sits above the work. The DIRECT band sits inside it, directly over pick rows someone is scanning.
  • One moving thing is what makes it special. If everything moves, nothing does.

So the band gets the same material without being the same kind of object: four discs as a number format — '"●●●○ "@' — the identical trick that already puts ▣ on a kit SKU and ▌ on that divider today. Zero bytes, zero images, moves with its own row automatically, and survives every insert and delete because it is the row.

Build order

Nothing applied yet
  1. The art. make-film.js renders both boards: flat #1a1a1a ground, unlit discs never drawn, 3px hex, area sampled, ground snapped past the rgb565 bug. Verified on the decoded frames of every one of the 261, not on the encoder's word.
  2. Upload and look, before anything else moves. Byte-verify on the VPS, re-install, then hard-reload the tab — a newly inserted over-grid image does not repaint in an open one, and getImages() lists it whether or not Sheets ever draws it. Only an eye can tell installed from visible.
  3. MASTHEAD.strip = false, push, re-run setupMasthead(). That hands D1 and E1 back and returns the strip's 539px to circulation. Fully reversible — it is the documented revert path.
  4. Split F1:H1 into F1:G1 and H1, and write the four readouts: 14 TO GRAB at 19px in D1, then the pulse, the floor and oldest at 11.5px. One size, weight and colour per cell. ⚠ E1 keeps a value containing h:mm AM/PM — ActivityLog.js regex-parses it for the board heartbeat, the sidebar pulse and /status, and a test fails if that ever becomes a move rather than a mirror.
  5. Then, separately, the discs on the DIRECT band — a number format, not an image. Never bundle a look with a repair; that rule is why the 31 August round lost three good fixes along with the design.

Steps 1–2 change nothing on the sheet that removeBanner() cannot undo. Step 3 is the first one that writes cells, and its revert is the same switch in the other direction.
